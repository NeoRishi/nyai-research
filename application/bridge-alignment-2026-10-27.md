# Bridge alignment, author verification and remaining application requirements

Checked against official MATS Residency pages on 27 October 2026. This is an application preparation document, not a submitted application, eligibility determination or prediction of acceptance.

## Project positioning

NyAI investigates when an AI research conclusion remains warranted by the execution evidence available to an overseer. Its immediate target is the gap between an evaluation having run and the resulting claim remaining applicable after changes to artifacts, scope, permissions or contrary evidence. The intended contribution is a reproducible diagnostic and failure analysis, with conventional verification as a serious baseline. Improved reviewer outcomes and superiority remain unestablished.

This direction connects to checkable automated alignment research and evidence-grounded assurance. The proposed safety pathway is to prevent unsupported or invalidated research conclusions from propagating into consequential safety decisions. It depends on valid evaluations, trustworthy observations, adequate evidence coverage and actual use of the oversight mechanism; it does not demonstrate reduced catastrophic risk. The underlying philosophical influence supplies questions about warrants and defeaters rather than empirical proof.

Bridge could supply domain feedback, task design expertise and collaborators for testing the unresolved oversight hypothesis. Whether Bridge is the appropriate personal transition path depends on the applicant's actual prior expertise, goals and constraints, which must be supplied and verified by the applicant. This document invents none of those facts.

## Critical reviewer assessment

The strongest reasons to reject this sample are substantive: the mechanism may duplicate established safeguards; constructed cases and labels share an AI author; complete revalidation may tie NyAI; no human oversight advantage is demonstrated; and evidence of the applicant's own research judgment remains incomplete. A reviewer may also find the safety theory of change too indirect or the tasks too simple to support generalisation.

Mitigations should be evidence-based: acknowledge close prior art, include a complete conventional baseline, retain failures, distinguish source-backed rules from human judgments, expose raw artifacts, and solicit independent challenge. Better formatting, framework adoption or a higher count of correlated templates cannot resolve these concerns.

## Focused continuation agenda and pivot criteria

The next presentation study has two testable hypotheses: at a fixed review budget, explicit records reduce unwarranted endorsement compared with ordinary logs; and any benefit does not come from excessive withholding of valid results. Measure decision errors, completion/withholding, correction quality and elapsed review time separately. Choose the minimum worthwhile difference and acceptable false-withhold margin prospectively with task experts, then size the study accordingly. If conditions merely execute the same deterministic predicate, do not call their tie a test of these reviewer hypotheses.

The following are proposed milestones, not a powered study design, frozen protocol or completed outcomes. Numerical targets and exclusion rules must be frozen before any confirmatory run; feasibility feedback may change them prospectively.

**First 30 days:** develop a reviewed rubric across several genuinely distinct research task families; capture execution, dataset, harness and claim-scope identity; include benign and withholding cases. Measure missing evidence fields and reviewer disagreements. If reference decisions remain ambiguous, revise the rubric before scoring methods. Require complete manifest capture for every retained case; report failures and exclusions rather than silently repairing them.

**Days 31–60:** compare complete conventional verification against the same evidence with warrants/revision records on held-out task families. Separate unwarranted acceptance, valid acceptance, appropriate withholding, coverage and computational effort. If the conditions are decision-equivalent, stop claiming decision superiority and investigate whether diagnostic localisation or reviewer effort is the only plausible benefit. If missing dependency edges bypass admission, move the final safety boundary to complete revalidation rather than rationalising graph-only admission.

**Days 61–90:** subject to authorisation and participants, run a counterbalanced reviewer pilot comparing ordinary logs with explicit records. Predefine the smallest worthwhile improvement, acceptable loss of valid acceptance, treatment of timeouts and uncertainty analysis before data collection. Record both review error rates and time. If results are inconclusive, report that; if records increase effort without an accuracy benefit, pivot to a failure taxonomy or evidence-quality benchmark rather than further interface expansion.

Across all stages, a shared-author oracle is not independent human validation, a test pass is not proof of safety, and zero observed failures is not a population guarantee. Any new paid model experiment, human study or publication needs its own authorised scope. Existing API approvals must not be silently expanded.
